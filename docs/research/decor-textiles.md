# decor-textiles cluster (throw blankets)
- Standard throw sizes: 50x60 in is a lap throw; 50x70 in covers a seated adult plus feet; 60x80 in is oversized for two people or a sofa drape.
- Fabric families: microfiber fleece (polyester, light, cheap, pills over time), sherpa (fleece one side, fuzzy pile other side, warm and shedding), faux fur (heavy pile, sheds and mats), chenille and chunky knit (textured, snags), cotton and wool (breathable, heavier, costlier).
- GSM (grams per square meter) shows fabric weight; 200 to 300 GSM is light fleece, 400 plus GSM is heavy; a blend of 240 and 230 GSM sherpa layers is mid weight.
- Pilling happens on polyester fleece after repeated washes; wash inside out on cold with similar fabrics, avoid fabric softener and high heat.
- Faux fur and sherpa shed fibers in the first washes; tumble dry on low with a dryer ball; matting from heat is permanent.
- Static builds up in dry winter air on polyester; dryer sheets help but coat fibers and reduce softness.
- Oeko-Tek Standard 100 certification means textiles tested for harmful substances; it is not a performance guarantee.
- Knit throws snag on jewelry and pet claws; lay flat to dry to avoid stretching.
- Throw on a sofa: draping over the back or arm looks styled; fold in thirds over the arm for a clean look.
- Color: neutral beige, gray, cream suit most sofas; patterned or striped add texture; dark colors hide pet hair poorly.
- Pet owners prefer tight weaves that shed less hair; sherpa and faux fur collect fur.
- Machine washability: most polyester throws are machine washable; chenille and chunky knits often need gentle or hand wash; check the label.
- Weight and warmth do not always match: thick faux fur is warm but traps heat, thin fleece with low GSM is cooler for year round use.
- Edges: bound or blanket stitched edges resist fraying; raw cut edges unravel.
- Packaging smells: new polyester blankets often need airing or a first wash.

## throw pillow inserts
- Insert size should be about 2 in larger than the cover for a full look (20 in insert in an 18 in cover); same size looks limp at the corners.
- Standard sizes: 16, 18, 20, 22, 24 and 26 in squares; 18 and 20 in are the most common sofa sizes; 26 in is a euro sham size for beds.
- Polyester fiberfill (virgin or down alternative) is hypoallergenic, cheap, washable; it clumps and flattens over a year or two.
- Down and feather inserts are loftier and can be karate-chopped; they cost more, shed quills and need spot care.
- Vacuum-packed inserts arrive flat; air out 24 to 48 hours, fluff by hand, or tumble on low with dryer balls 5 to 10 minutes.
- Insert shell fabric matters: tight-weave cotton or brushed polyester blocks fiber leaks; loose shells let fill poke through zippers and seams.
- Firm fill supports the back; soft fill suits decor and bed layering; dense inserts hold shape for years.
- Piping or sewn edges give crisp corners; plain seams round off.
- Machine washable inserts should be washed gently, dried on low with a tennis ball or dryer balls, and fully dry to avoid mildew.
- Replace inserts when they stay flat after fluffing, usually every 2 to 4 years with daily use.

## peel and stick wallpaper
- Surface prep decides success: clean, dry, smooth walls; paint cured at least 2 to 4 weeks; textured walls, fresh paint and flat-finish or low-quality paint make adhesion fail or pull paint at removal.
- Rolls are sold in narrow widths (about 17 to 24 in) or full 20 to 28 in; narrow rolls are easier for one person but add more seams.
- Coverage per roll is listed as sq ft; add 10 to 15 percent for pattern match and trimming; measure wall height and width, subtract doors and windows.
- Pattern repeat and match type (straight, drop or random) decide waste; large repeats need extra rolls.
- Vinyl and PVC faces wipe clean; thin papers tear and stretch; thicker rolls (around 0.3 to 0.5 mm) hide wall flaws better.
- Tools: squeegee or plastic card, utility knife with fresh blade, level or plumb line; start at a corner or center with a plumb line.
- Seams show on dark patterns; overlap slightly or butt joints tightly; heat from a hair dryer eases corners and curves.
- Kitchens and bathrooms: steam and grease weaken adhesive; keep away from direct heat and splash zones like behind a stove.
- Removal: peel slowly at a low angle, warm with a hair dryer; flat-paint walls can lose paint; test a hidden patch first.
- Rentals: check lease; removable does not mean guaranteed damage free.
- Textured grasscloth looks are usually printed or embossed vinyl, not real fiber; embossing helps hide seams and bubbles.
- Roll in the room for a day before hanging so the vinyl relaxes; store flat or rolled loosely.
