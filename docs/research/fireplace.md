# fireplace cluster (fireplace screens, electric fireplace inserts, blanket ladders)
## screens
- Measure the opening width and height; screens should overlap the opening by a few inches each side; frame edges add 1 to 2 in.
- Mesh density: fine mesh stops embers but cuts some radiant heat; a pencil should not pass through the mesh. Around 4 mm squares is common on spark guards.
- Wood fires need a true spark guard; decorative single panels suit gas or electric or cold hearths.
- Three-panel folding screens stand on angled side panels; flat panels need wide base feet; fully flat folds tip more easily.
- Piano (full-length) hinges close gaps and resist pinching fingers; plain hinges leave gaps.
- Keep the screen in place whenever a wood fire burns; never leave unattended.
- Follow manufacturer clearance rules; commonly cited is about 12 in between a firebox and combustibles, but check your appliance and local code.
- A screen is not a child gate; it gets hot near a wood fire; use a proper hearth gate for toddlers if needed.
- Powder-coated steel resists rust; wrought iron frames are heavier and steadier; thin frames bend if bumped.
- Cleaning: let ashes cool, vacuum with a soft brush, wipe frame dry.
## electric inserts
- A 1500 W heater draws about 12.5 A at 120 V; keep it on its own circuit when possible; shared circuits with other heaters trip breakers.
- Heat is supplemental zone heat, often around 5,000 BTU, not whole-house heating.
- Measure the cavity width, height and depth including trim; many inserts need 8 in or more depth; generic trim kits can look flimsy.
- Keep wood and combustibles away from the heat discharge; follow the manual for clearance.
- Flame effects use LEDs and can run without heat; flame colors, ember beds, and brightness vary.
- Remote controls get lost; look for a panel on the unit as backup.
- LED life is long, but heater elements and fans wear; fan noise is common on cheap units.
- Many inserts are plug-in; hardwired models need an electrician.
- Glass front gets warm; pets and kids should be kept back.
## blanket ladders
- Leaning ladders need the right angle; a 1 to 3 in lean rail gap prevents sliding; check the floor so it does not slip.
- Typical sizes 5 to 6 ft tall, 4 to 5 rungs; rung spacing 8 to 12 in suits folded throws.
- Wood ladders (pine, bamboo, acacia) vs metal; wood is warmer but can scratch walls.
- Weight: a bulky throw can weigh 3 to 6 lb; five throws and pillows add up; check the rated capacity.
- Anti-slip feet and wall protection pads prevent damage.
- Also used as towel racks, plant shelves; do not use as a real ladder.
- Rubberwood and bamboo resist warping better than thin pine.
- Blankets folded in thirds drape evenly; rolled blankets look messy on ladders.
